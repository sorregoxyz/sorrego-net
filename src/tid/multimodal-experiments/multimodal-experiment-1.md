Lately, I've been wondering what happens to material if I stop only looking at surfaces and start pressing against them. Until now, the project has been built from photographs, squares of color and texture sampled from walls, pavements, bollards, and doors. Each card isolates a chromatic fragment and treats it as a specimen. But walking around the city, I kept thinking about the other side of these surfaces, the way they grip shoes, collect dust, catch water. So I bought a block of clay and tried a slight detour.

The first experiments happened at home. To conduct them, I used whatever was at hand: a silicone homepad, a kitchen tool, the veins of maize leaves, and the lids and handles of pots. I flattened the clay into small pads and pressed them firmly against each object. Once I inked them and later printed them in my sketchbook, the objects timidly reappeared as soft, lo-fi, ephemeral lithographies.
